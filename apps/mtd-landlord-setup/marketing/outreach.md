## Why there is no cold-email sequence in this file
Self-managing landlords are INDIVIDUAL subscribers under PECR reg 22. Unsolicited marketing email to individuals requires prior consent, and the soft opt-in doesn't apply (no prior sale or negotiation). So: zero cold email, zero scraped or purchased lists, zero "just one friendly note" exceptions — the sibling ventures' B2B sequences do NOT port here. Acquisition is (1) answer-first posts in landlord communities and (2) opt-in nurture for people who join the waitlist / free MTD pre-check form. Question pattern: "3 properties, everything in Excel, no accountant — which software? Every 'best MTD software' list contradicts the last one."

> Honest starting point: check the free routes before paying anyone anything.
>
> - If you bank with NatWest, RBS or Ulster, or open a free Mettle account, FreeAgent comes bundled free — check its current landlord/MTD Income Tax support fits your setup.

>
> The four questions that actually decide it: joint ownership (products split income very differently), property count (free tiers cap units), which bank you're with (a native feed beats a better app without one), and whether you also have self-employment income (some landlord-only tools can't carry it).

## Answer template 2 — "What's actually due on 7 August? Will I be fined?"
Question pattern: "First quarterly update — what do I actually send HMRC? And am I fined if it's late?"

> What's due: totals of rental income and expenses for 6 April–5 July, sent from digital records through HMRC-recognised software. It's not a tax return, no tax is payable at that point, and the figures are cumulative — a Q1 mistake corrects itself in Q2.
>
> The honest penalty answer: HMRC is not issuing penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026/27 while the system beds in (LITRG covers this well). Anyone selling you fine-panic for 7 August hasn't read the guidance. The real cost of skipping is workload: miss Q1 and the 7 November update covers six months of reconstruction instead of three, and January's year-end still has to reconcile all of it.
>
> What IS required regardless: digital records from 6 April 2026 in recognised software, or a properly bridged spreadsheet — a bare spreadsheet alone no longer satisfies the rules.
>
> Disclosure: I sell a 48h done-for-you setup, so weigh that — the full what's-due breakdown is free here: {{guide: mtd-landlords-first-quarterly-7-august}}.

## Answer template 3 — "Jointly owned with my wife/husband — do we BOTH need software?"
Question pattern: "House in joint names. One MTD account or two? Her income is under £50k anyway?"

> Thresholds are per person: qualifying income is each owner's share of GROSS rents (before expenses) plus any self-employment turnover. Married couples/civil partners default to a 50/50 split unless a valid Form 17 declaration says otherwise — so one of you can be mandated now while the other isn't until the £30,000 wave in April 2027.
>
> If both are mandated: two sets of digital records, two software setups, two quarterly updates per deadline. There's no joint filing, and a shared spreadsheet has to be split consistently or the two returns won't reconcile.
>
> There's also a joint-property easement — broadly, share-of-income only in the quarterly updates, expense detail annually — worth using, but it must be configured deliberately in both owners' software and checked against current gov.uk guidance.
>
> Disclosure: I run a paid setup service and joint ownership is half our intakes — the quirks are written up free here: {{guide: mtd-joint-property-landlords}}.
