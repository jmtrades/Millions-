# MeetingPacks — 5 @theoperator short-form scripts

Format per script: HOOK (0-2s) → TENSION (2-15s) → PROOF/DEMO (15-40s) → CTA (link in bio / pinned).
Standing rule: every claim on screen carries its source on screen. The audience is burned agency founders — they fact-check.

## 1. The price gap — "£119 vs £2,800 a month"
HOOK: "I charge £119 per meeting. Agencies charge £2,800 a month for the same thing — whether the meetings happen or not."
TENSION: And it gets worse: SaaStr surveyed 1,200+ companies about outsourced SDRs. 7% said it really worked. Sixty-seven percent said it didn't. You know what never failed? The invoice.
PROOF: Screen-record the 2026 pricing guides ($3k–$14k/mo, setup fees on top) and the SaaStr post. Then my pricing page: £595 for 5 qualified meetings that showed. No retainer field exists because there is no retainer.
CTA: "Pack link in bio. If checkout's closed, slots are full — that's the point."

## 2. The teardown — "Watch the machine research one prospect"
HOOK: "This is what £0.05 of AI research on one prospect looks like. No agency SDR does this — they don't have time."
TENSION: Screen: a real prospect (name blurred). The research pass pulls their case studies, pricing tier, hiring page, a founder post from March. Cost counter running in the corner: pennies. An SDR paid $60k a year gives this prospect 90 seconds.
PROOF: The email it produces — one specific, checkable observation per line, no {{first_name}} template smell. Then the reply that email got. "Batch APIs plus prompt caching cut this to ~25% of list price. That cost collapse is the entire business model."
CTA: "We do this to ~1,000 prospects per pack. £119 per meeting that shows. Bio."

## 3. The mistake — "You bought meetings without defining 'meeting'"
HOOK: "The #1 documented dispute in pay-per-appointment isn't fraud. It's that nobody wrote down what 'qualified' means."
TENSION: Vendor books an 'exploring options' intern. Bills it. You refuse. Everyone's furious, nobody's wrong — there was no definition. Industry guides call misaligned qualification definitions the most common source of disputes, and 10–30% of booked meetings no-show on top.
PROOF: Show the MeetingPacks intake form: ICP filters + explicit interest + booked AND attended — approved in writing BEFORE the first send. Highlight the line: "No-shows never count against your pack."
CTA: "The definition template is free in the guide (bio). The delivery is £595."

## 4. The receipts — "This meeting was booked by the thing it sells"
HOOK: "Every discovery call on this calendar was booked by the product it's about. The demo IS the meeting."
TENSION: If I cold-call you about cold outreach and my own outreach didn't work on you, why would you pay me? Every SDR agency has this paradox. Most solve it with ads.
PROOF: Calendar view (names blurred): meetings this week, each tagged with the sequence email that booked it. Open one thread: the research artifact the email cited, the prospect's actual reply — "honestly this is the first cold email I've read to the end." That's the pitch, verbatim, from a buyer.
CTA: "If my email found you the way I'd find your buyers, you've already seen the product work. £595 for 5. Bio."

## 5. Build-in-public — "The real economics of a £595 pack"
HOOK: "I sell 5 meetings for £595. Here's the full cost side, live, including the part where I can lose money."
TENSION: Nobody publishes this. Watch: verified leads £30–80. AI research £10–100 depending on model class. Inbox and domain infra £30–60/mo amortized. Sending platform ~£10. Cash COGS £80–250 against £595 — 58–87% gross margin at average-or-better reply rates.
PROOF: The dashboard, warts included: packs sold, emails per meeting this week, the no-show I ate (never billed), and the catch — at bottom-quartile reply rates a pack needs 3,000+ emails and I lose money on labor. Which is why I refused {{N}} intakes this month and why checkout closes when slots fill. Refusing bad-fit packs isn't a growth problem, it's the margin.
CTA: "Numbers again next week either way. Pack in bio — if it's open."
