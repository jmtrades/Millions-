## R2 Timing Gate
Verdict: PASS (verified 2026-07-10, fresh web searches).
- Annual board report requirement CONFIRMED: under the Consumer Duty (Principle 12 / PRIN 2A), a firm's governing body must review and approve an assessment of whether the firm is delivering good outcomes at least annually; first reports were due 31 July 2024 and the requirement recurs annually. Sources: https://www.braithwate.com/consumer-duty-board-report ; https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/consumer-duty-board-reports-good-practice-areas-improvement
- 31 July 2026 as the next report date CONFIRMED as industry-standard anniversary: "next one being completed by 31st July 2026" (https://insurancecompliance.co.uk/services/consumer-duty-board-report); Oxera frames "the 2026 editions" (https://www.oxera.com/insights/agenda/articles/consumer-duty-board-reports-what-is-required-for-the-2026-editions/); retainer provider threesixty runs "Are you ready for the 31 July deadlines?" member comms (https://www.threesixtyservices.co.uk/article/14340). Nuance: the rule is "at least annually" from the firm's last approval, not a statutory universal 31 July — but the 31 July 2024 first deadline clustered anniversaries on/before 31 July, so urgency is real for the bulk of the market.
- 24 Feb 2026 FCA update CONFIRMED: FCA updated its Consumer Duty board reports good/poor-practice webpage on 24 February 2026 with, for the first time, insights for SMALLER firms (governance via a knowledgeable "critical friend"; external experts incl. trade bodies "at proportionate cost"; proportionate treatment of limited MI). Sources: https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/02/fca-updates-consumer-duty-board-reports-webpage-by-providing-further-insights-for-smaller-firms/ ; https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/consumer-duty-board-reports-good-practice-areas-improvement ; commentary: https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102mm22/consumer-duty-board-reports-one-size-does-not-fit-small-firms
- Continued FCA scrutiny CONFIRMED: FCA blog "Year 2 Consumer Duty Board Reports: progress and what comes next" (https://www.fca.org.uk/news/blogs/year-2-consumer-duty-board-reports-progress-and-what-comes-next); the FCA has begun contacting firms to request completed assessments (https://www.threesixtyservices.co.uk/article/14343).
- TTFD implication: deadline is 21 days from today (2026-07-10 → 2026-07-31). Hard urgency window exists but is brutally short for a cold-start trust sale; missing it parks demand for ~11 months.

## R4 Red Team
Strongest failure case — the trust/accountability chokepoint, compressed into 21 days:
- The board report is a personally-owned governance document: the governing body must review, approve, and confirm compliance, and the FCA is actively requesting completed reports from firms (https://www.threesixtyservices.co.uk/article/14343). A principal must hand complaints logs, client-file samples and vulnerable-customer records to an unregulated solo engineer with no FCA track record, no compliance PI cover, and a landing page — then sign the output personally. That is a hard trust gate a £449 Stripe checkout does not clear at 21 days' notice.
- Searchable fact: the FCA's review of 180 firms' first board reports (published 11 Dec 2024) flagged as poor practice precisely what a fast external AI-draft over thin MI produces: "insufficient data quality to justify conclusions", high-level generic claims (e.g. "products are designed to meet the needs of the target market" without stating them), MI presented "without adequate explanations", catch-all vulnerability categories. Sources: https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/consumer-duty-board-reports-good-practice-areas-improvement ; https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/eu/fca-findings-following-review-into-firms-approaches-to-completing-the-first-annual-consumer-duty-board-report/ ; https://www.globalfinregblog.com/2024/12/fca-publishes-further-consumer-duty-feedback-for-firms/
- "Raw MI in" dependency: the FCA's own finding is that many firms' underlying data was inadequate — the desperate late buyers 3 weeks from deadline are disproportionately the firms whose MI cannot be packaged into a defensible report without remediation work the £449 price cannot fund.
- Seasonality: one hard deadline/year; the £99/mo year-round plan is unproven mitigation.
Strongest rebuttal:
- The FCA's 24 Feb 2026 smaller-firm update EXPLICITLY legitimises the category: smaller firms "may benefit from a knowledgeable 'critical friend'" and "external experts... where available at proportionate cost, can be helpful" (https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/02/fca-updates-consumer-duty-board-reports-webpage-by-providing-further-insights-for-smaller-firms/). The regulator itself tells this buyer to seek proportionate-cost external help — that is rare, citable licence for the offer.
- The deliverable is drafting labour + clause-by-clause mapping to the FCA's published rubric — not regulated advice, not assurance; the board keeps ownership of conclusions. Clause-mapping directly remediates the #1 flagged deficiency (MI without explanation). Unregulated template sellers already transact in this market (see R3 table), proving firms buy compliance documents from non-authorised vendors.
Defensible: TRUE — narrowly. The rebuttal answers the regulatory-legitimacy objection with the FCA's own words, but does NOT manufacture personal credibility for a no-name founder inside a closed professional community on a 21-day clock; that residual risk is priced into reach0/ttfd/fit below, not treated as a kill.
