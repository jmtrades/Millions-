## R2 Timing Gate
**Verdict: PASS — spec's dates verified fresh as of 2026-07-10; no further delay has landed.**
- Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 (Council sign-off 18 Dec 2025; trilogue deal 4 Dec; EP adoption 11 Dec 2025) postpones application to **30 Dec 2026 for large AND medium operators/traders** and **30 Jun 2027 for micro/small enterprises and natural persons** — exactly as the R1 spec claims. https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/18/deforestation-council-signs-off-targeted-revision-to-simplify-and-postpone-the-regulation/ ; https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251211IPR32168/deforestation-law-parliament-adopts-changes-to-postpone-and-simplify-measures ; https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation
- Simplifications in the final act: only the operator **first placing** product on the EU market submits a full DDS; downstream operators/traders no longer file their own DDS but must collect/retain upstream DDS reference numbers (non-SME downstream must register in the Information System); micro/small get simplified traceability + one streamlined declaration. https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/04/eu-deforestation-law-council-and-parliament-reach-a-deal-on-targeted-revision/ ; https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-amended-eudr-what-has-changed-and-what-has-remained
- **2026 status check (post-cutoff risk cleared):** Commission published its mandated simplification review **4 May 2026** with updated guidance/FAQ + a draft Delegated Act on product scope (adds soluble coffee, some palm-oil derivatives, frozen cattle tongues; removes retreaded tyres, hides/leather). It explicitly signals the EUDR **"will not be reopened"** and existing timelines apply; the 'no-risk' country tier was ruled out (three tiers stay). No third postponement exists as of July 2026. https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/eu-commission-publishes-simplification-review-of-eudr ; https://www.globalelr.com/2026/05/european-commission-releases-new-eu-deforestation-regulation-measures/ ; https://www.adherent.com/blog/eudr-simplification-review-2026-whats-new-what-stays-the-same-and-what-additional-support-can-companies-expect/ ; https://osapiens.com/blog/eudr-update-april-2026/
- Timing edge is real: ~5.5 months to the large/medium deadline, and supplier geolocation collection "can take months" (https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-importers/), so July 2026 is the forced buying season. Caveat: EUDR has slipped twice before — political risk is not zero, but the freshest official signal is "no reopening."
- Watch-out the spec got right vs. per-consignment claim: DDS duty now sits ONLY with the first-placer (the spec's importer buyer IS the first-placer, so the offer survives the downstream exemption).

## R4 Red Team
**Strongest failure case:** EUDR liability cannot be outsourced — the operator "bears full legal responsibility" and faces fines of **at least 4% of total annual EU-wide turnover**, product confiscation and market bans (https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-penalties-what-non-compliance-actually-costs-fines-bans-criminal-liability ; https://tracextech.com/eudr-due-diligence/). A buyer betting a 4%-of-turnover penalty will vet providers for track record and liability cover — and trusted incumbents already sell the identical DFY promise: customs broker ASD Group Customs handles the "full process… electronic submission via TRACES-NT" (https://asdgroupcustoms.com/due-diligence-statement-dds-under-the-eudr/), Intertek sells EUDR assurance, and INTEGRAC already occupies the coffee/cocoa readiness niche (https://integrac.eu/). A solo UK founder with zero supply-chain credibility loses that trust contest, and compliance procurement (references, DPAs, insurance questions) does not close inside a 14-day cash window. Compounding: the UK-heavy channel (BCA/TTF) is diluted because EUDR only binds placement on the EU market — GB-only importers are out of scope — and certified supply chains increasingly get geolocation free upstream (Fairtrade Plot Insights, 800+ co-ops: https://www.comunicaffe.com/fairtrade-plot-insights-eudr-coffee-cocoa-cooperatives/), i.e., medium operators can lean on co-ops'/certifiers' systems for the data-chasing half of the offer.
**Strongest rebuttal:** The deadline is now credibly immovable — the Commission's 4 May 2026 review states the EUDR "will not be reopened" (https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/eu-commission-publishes-simplification-review-of-eudr) — while supplier mapping/data collection "can take months" (https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-importers/) and smallholder co-ops largely cannot self-serve geolocation (https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/coffee/tips-become-eudr-compliant). The 25–250-staff importer is priced out of enterprise tools (Coolset entry ~€12k/yr) yet too busy for €29-79/mo DIY software; a £999 fixed-scope readiness audit is a low-liability wedge purchase (diagnostic, not delegation of the DDS), which sidesteps the trust barrier for the first transaction and converts to the £499/mo retainer only after demonstrated competence. TTF's directory alone lists 370+ member companies (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber_Trade_Federation) plus BCA's public member list (https://britishcoffeeassociation.org/current-members/) — enough at-bats without the cold list.
**Defensible: FALSE.** No structural moat: the service is replicable by any customs broker or software vendor's managed tier; accumulated client plot data legally belongs to the operator (5-yr retention duty); trust assets accrue to incumbents. Survivable via speed + niche focus, not defensibility.
