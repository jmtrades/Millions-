# Pay-Per-Meeting Outbound Engine
Category: AI-service arbitrage | Status: R1-generated 2026-07-10

## R1 Spec
- Offer: Done-for-you outbound: we build the ICP list, deep-research every prospect, write and send 1,000 tailored emails/mo from managed inboxes, and handle replies — the buyer pays only for qualified meetings that actually show on their calendar. Undercuts $3–14k/mo SDR agencies and removes their core failure mode (paying retainers for meetings that never convert). ~85% AI-automated (research agents, reply classification/handling), human review of edge-case replies.
- Buyer: Founder of B2B productized service or boutique agency (recruitment, dev shop, RevOps, design subscription), 1–15 staff, UK/US, ACV £2k–£30k, previously burned by an SDR agency or DIY-ing outbound.
- Price: £595 for a 5-qualified-meeting pack (£119/meeting), no retainer; inbox infrastructure included. Bought via Stripe from landing page; async onboarding (ICP form + calendar link).
- Channel to first 10: No dependence on the 692K/30K lists. Existing Skool community (agency owners are the exact ICP) + @theoperator audience; proof-first angle: every discovery call the founder takes was itself booked by the engine — "this meeting is the product." £0 spend.
- Timing hypothesis: (a) Buyer dissatisfaction is documented and quantified in 2026: outsourced SDRs run $3,000–$14,000/mo and a SaaStr survey found only 7% of companies say outsourced SDRs "really worked" (67% failed) — an incumbent priced for disruption; (b) per-prospect deep research + personalization cost collapsed to ~£0.01 via agentic web research with 50% batch-API and up-to-90% prompt-caching discounts (GPT-5.4 batch at $1.25/$7.50 per 1M tokens) — research-grade personalization at 1,000-prospect scale was not available as cheap API primitives in mid-2025.

## R2 Timing Gate
Verdict: PASS (verified 2026-07-10, hostile review)

**Leg (a) — incumbent price + documented dissatisfaction: VERIFIED.**
- Outsourced SDR programs run $3,000–$14,000/mo in 2026, confirmed across multiple independent 2026 pricing guides ([Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost), [Whistle](https://www.whistle.ltd/post/outsourced-sdr-pricing-guide-2026), [RevNew](https://revnew.com/blog/sdr-outsourcing-cost), [ORRJO](https://orrjo.com/outsourced-sdr-cost-guide)). Setup fees $3k–$5k are common; hybrid models run $3k–$8k retainer PLUS $100–$300 per qualified appointment ([ORRJO](https://orrjo.com/outsourced-sdr-cost-guide)).
- SaaStr survey (1,200+ respondents): only 7% say outsourced SDRs "really worked," 26% "sort of worked" — i.e., 67% failed ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/only-7-of-you-have-really-gotten-outsourced-sdrs-to-work/); echoed by [TechCXO](https://www.techcxo.com/insights-sdr-agency-success-rate/), [DeltaV](https://deltav-global.com/the-truth-about-sdr-outsourcing/)). The "67% didn't work" claim in the R1 spec is accurate.

**Leg (b) — per-prospect AI research cost collapse: VERIFIED directionally; £0.01 is the optimistic bound.**
- Batch APIs give 50% off all major models; prompt caching gives up to 90% off cached input (Anthropic cache reads −90%, OpenAI −50%); the discounts stack to roughly 25% of standard per-call cost ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/llm-api-pricing-comparison/), [DevToolLab](https://devtoollab.com/blog/prompt-caching-guide), [ZenVanRiel](https://zenvanriel.com/ai-engineer-blog/llm-api-cost-comparison-2026/)).
- Honest characterization: ~£0.01/prospect holds only with small/cheap models; frontier-model deep research (e.g., Sonnet-class at $3/$15 per 1M, batched) is nearer £0.05–£0.10/prospect for a 30–50K-token research pass. Either figure is 2–3 orders of magnitude below human SDR research cost, so the unit economics claim survives even at the pessimistic bound.

**Counter-trend check — falling reply rates: REAL, and it cuts both ways.**
- Instantly's 2026 benchmark report: average cold-email reply rate 3.43%, down from ~5% (2025) and 8.5% (2019); top quartile 5.5%, elite campaigns >10% ([Instantly 2026 report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026), [Reachoutly](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/response-rate/)). Drivers: inbox saturation, Google/Yahoo/Microsoft bulk-sender enforcement 2024–25, AI-slop trust deficit. Tight, genuinely personalized systems still report 10–18% ([Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/)).
- Do the economics still work at 3.43%? Model: 1,000 emails → ~34 replies → ~20–30% positive → 7–10 interested → ~50% book → 4–5 booked → 10–30% no-show → ~3–4 shows → qualification filter → ~2.5–4 qualified shows per 1,000 emails at AVERAGE performance. A 5-meeting pack therefore needs ~1,300–2,000 emails at average craft, ~700–1,000 at top-quartile. Cash COGS per pack (verified leads £30–80, AI research £10–100, inbox/domain infra £30–60/mo amortized, sending platform ~£10) ≈ £80–250 against £595 revenue → 58–87% gross cash margin. Verdict: economics work at average-or-better 2026 rates for an operator whose craft is cold email; at bottom-quartile reply rates a pack takes 3,000+ emails and the model loses money on labor. The decline is simultaneously the mechanism (it is why $3–14k retainers feel indefensible to buyers) and the tail risk (founder absorbs it — see R4).

## R4 Red Team
**Strongest failure case (evidenced):** Pure pay-per-qualified-show pricing concentrates every documented failure mode of this industry onto a solo founder. (1) He absorbs 100% of channel risk in a channel decaying 8.5%→3.43% avg replies since 2019 ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)) — incumbents charge retainers precisely to socialize that variance, and even Belkins hedges its PPA option with $300–$800/meeting and $10k minimums ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/appointment-setting-costs-pricing-models)). (2) The #1 documented conflict in pay-per-appointment is the qualification dispute: "the most common source of disputes... is a misaligned definition of what constitutes a qualified appointment," plus 10–30% no-show leakage the vendor eats on pay-per-show ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/pay-per-appointment-lead-generation), [LeadsHook](https://www.leadshook.com/blog/pay-per-appointment/), [SuperhumanProspecting](https://superhumanprospecting.com/drawbacks-pay-per-appointment-lead-generation-services/)) — at £119/meeting there is almost no buffer to absorb a disputed or refunded meeting, and the ICP is by definition burned-and-distrustful buyers primed to dispute. (3) Volume incentives are documented to degrade quality ("vendors who deliver junk are responding rationally to a contract that rewards volume" — [Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/b2b-lead-generation-pay-for-performance)), and 20 clients ≈ £12k/mo revenue but 20 concurrent multi-niche campaigns (list, infra, replies, QA) — solo ops hell. (4) 11x/Monaco-class incumbents have $35–74M and sales teams to bundle outcome guarantees if the wedge works.

**Strongest rebuttal:** Every documented failure driver above is an incentive/cost-structure problem of HUMAN agencies at volume. The candidate's cost base (research at pennies via batch+cache, founder is an elite cold-email operator for whom top-quartile 5.5%+/10–18% reply systems are documented and attainable — [Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/)) lets him profitably deliver at a price incumbents structurally cannot match. Dispute risk is mitigable by contract: written qualification definition (decision-maker + ICP match + attended = billable; no-shows never billed — already the offer's design), and prepaid packs reverse the cashflow risk that kills performance-priced vendors. Downside is bounded per-pack (£595), not per-retainer; capacity can be capped at a client count one operator can serve.

**Defensible: TRUE (barely).** Each leg of the failure case has a specific, evidenced mitigation and per-pack bounded downside; the unbounded risk (multi-niche ops load) is capacity-limited rather than existential. This is a thin-moat grind business, not a venture-scale one — but it survives a kill gate.
