## R2 Timing Gate

Verdict: **FAIL → KILL-R2** (gated 2026-07-10, 3 web searches)

### Claim 1: "'AI Operations Manager' emerged as mainstream 2026 hire, $130k avg" — CONFLATED
- The $130k figure is real but misread. Wellfound's page is salary data for **Operations Managers employed AT AI startups** ($130k avg, $180k NYC) — i.e., ops people working inside AI companies — NOT companies hiring an "AI Operations Manager" to run their AI rollout. The R1 hook ("Companies are paying $130k+ for 'AI Operations Managers'") is built on this conflation. https://wellfound.com/hiring-data/r/operations-manager-2/i/artificial-intelligence
- Partial rescue: Glassdoor does list "AI Operations" as a salary category, $90k–$140k range — the title has a real footprint, but at nothing like "mainstream hire at 20–200 person companies" scale. https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/ai-operations-salary-SRCH_KO0,13.htm
- "Emergent role" support is think-piece grade (thecasehq, nucamp — R1's own citations), not hiring data showing SMBs externally hiring this title over incumbent ops managers.

### Claim 2: "90%+ of leaders budgeting AI tools/upskilling in 2026" — UNVERIFIED AS STATED
- No source found stating 90%+ of leaders budgeting AI **upskilling** in 2026. Nearest real stats, all materially different in scope or horizon:
  - McKinsey: **92% of companies plan to increase AI investment over the next three years** — investment broadly, not upskilling; a 3-year horizon, not a 2026 budget line. https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential-at-work
  - KPMG Global AI Pulse (Mar 2026): **three out of four (75%)** global leaders will prioritize AI investment. https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/03/kpmg-global-ai-pulse-survey.html
  - Upskilling-specific: AI-leader orgs allocate **~60% of AI budget** to upskilling/retraining — a budget-share stat, not a 90% prevalence stat. https://iternal.ai/ai-skills-gap
- The 90%+ claim appears to be a mangled recollection of McKinsey's 92%-investment-over-3-years stat.

### Honest answer: timing edge or evergreen demand?
**Evergreen upskilling demand wearing a new-job-title costume.** AI-upskilling demand has run continuously since 2023 and is still CEO-led and surging in 2026 (BCG AI Radar 2026: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/as-ai-investments-surge-ceos-take-the-lead) — that is a demand signal, not a timing edge. The "~12-month window before the role is hired over ops people's heads" is asserted nowhere in any source; McKinsey's explicitly 3-year investment horizon undercuts the urgency mechanism. The genuinely-new-in-2026 element (a job title with a salary benchmark) is a marketing hook, not a window. Per gate rule (wrong/vague → kill): one headline stat is conflated, the other fails verification at stated scope, and the window mechanism is unevidenced. **KILL-R2.**
