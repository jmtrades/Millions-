# FlatRef (Agent-to-Agent Referral Exchange)
Category: Marketplace | Status: R1-generated 2026-07-10

## R1 Spec
- Offer: Flat-subscription agent-to-agent referral board for US realtors: post outbound referrals (relocating clients, out-of-area buyers/sellers) and claim inbound ones, keeping the standard ~25% agent-to-agent referral fee between agents with ZERO platform take — vs Zillow Flex/Preferred and referral portals taking 25–40% of gross commission at closing. On one $10K GCI closing, a Zillow-style 35% fee is $3,500; the sub costs $59. Free side: agents posting outbound referrals (they earn the referral fee, so posting is free and incentivized).
- Buyer (paying side): US residential real estate agents and team leads (NAR members, 2+ yrs licensed) who want inbound referral deal flow without surrendering 25–40% of commission. ASSUMED GEOGRAPHY: the 30K real-estate email list is assumed to be US realtors — verify by sampling 500 records (domains/addresses) before launch; if it proves UK, run the channel purely via US agent Facebook referral groups and pivot the list to candidate m1-rra-landlord-switch-leads.
- Price: $59/mo per agent (or $499/yr); posting outbound referrals free forever.
- Channel to first 10: Email blast to the 30K list (assumed US realtors) offering free founding-member posting + paid claiming; parallel manual posting in the large public "Referrals — Agent to Agent" Facebook groups (several exceed 100K members) where this behavior already happens unstructured. £0/$0 spend.
- Supply seeding: Referral postings already exist and are aggregatable: week 1 feed built by aggregating/structuring public referral requests from agent-to-agent Facebook groups (with poster permission via DM) + free-posting blast to the 30K list. No cold start: paying agents see a live board before buying.
- Timing hypothesis: Post-NAR-settlement rules (mandatory written buyer agreements; no MLS-advertised co-compensation) forced buyer agents to justify every dollar, while the 2026 Zillow Flex antitrust lawsuit spotlights agents surrendering 25–40% of gross commission on portal referrals — an active, dated fee revolt creating demand for flat-fee referral routing. Searchable: "Zillow Flex lawsuit 2026", "NAR settlement buyer agreement".

## R2 Timing Gate
Verdict: PASS (drivers verified and dated), with a material caveat that caps the timing score.

Agent Stephanie Dupuis (Silverdale, WA) sued Zillow alleging Sherman Act violations — commission fees "up to 40% when the industry norm is 25%", illegal tying of lead access to Zillow Home Loans, and retaliation; seeks class status for all US agents in Premier/Preferred/Flex programs. https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/01/20/zillow-hit-with-another-lawsuit-over-allegations-of-steering ; https://www.rismedia.com/2026/01/20/zillow-named-in-another-antitrust-lawsuit-real-estate-agent-alleges-steering-to-home-loans-business/ ; https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/jan/22/zillow-accused-of-referral-monopoly-steering-homeb/ ; https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-lawsuit-preferred-agent/ . Plaintiff firm Keller Rohrback is actively recruiting agent claimants ("Tired of paying Zillow's excessive fees... You may have a legal claim"): https://www.kellerrohrback.com/news/zillow-excessive-fees-litigation . Related suit names eXp as supporting Zillow (Apr 2026): https://www.inman.com/2026/04/17/buyers-accuse-exp-of-supporting-zillows-fraudulent-business/
- Post-NAR pressure: VERIFIED, dated Aug 17, 2024 (still binding in 2026). Written buyer agreements required before touring; compensation must be conspicuously disclosed, objectively ascertainable, not open-ended; no MLS co-comp. https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs ; https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101 . Buyer-agent fee is now an explicit negotiated line item (~2.4% avg late 2025): https://www.onepercentlistings.com/post/how-real-estate-commission-works-in-2025
- Honest answer to the urgency question: the litigation is primarily DISCOURSE for this product, not a purchase trigger. (a) The lawsuit's urgent call-to-action for an aggrieved agent is to join the class action (free, potential payout via Keller Rohrback), not to pay $59/mo for a board. (b) Category mismatch: Zillow Flex fees are charged on Zillow-GENERATED CONSUMER leads; FlatRef offers agent-to-agent referrals and sources zero consumer demand — an agent quitting Flex replaces it with other consumer-lead sources, not an A2A board. The suit validates fee resentment (real, dated) and is a usable cold-email hook, but the causal chain "Zillow sued → agent pays $59/mo for A2A board" is weak. Passes because both drivers are real, dated, and correctly characterized on the fee facts (25-40% take corroborated by the complaint coverage above and by OpCity/ReadyConnect's 30-35% at-close fees: https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-opcitys-referral-fee/). Timing scored 5, not higher, for the discourse-vs-urgency gap.
