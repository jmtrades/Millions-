# LandlordSwitch
Category: Marketplace | Status: R1-generated 2026-07-10

## R1 Spec
- Offer: Exclusive lead feed for UK letting agents: self-managing landlords who, post-Renters' Rights Act, want full-management quotes or a sale valuation. Leads captured via a free "RRA compliance risk scorer + Information Sheet proof-of-delivery tool" (the Act required serving the official info sheet to every existing tenant by 31 May 2026, with fines up to £7,000 per tenancy). A converted full-management landlord is worth ~12–15% of rent ≈ £1,800–£2,700/yr/property to the agent; a sale instruction worth ~1% of sale price. Free side: landlords (free compliance tooling + quotes).
- Buyer (paying side): UK letting agent branch managers/owners (independent agencies, 1–10 branches) seeking full-management instructions; secondary payer: estate agents wanting landlord-seller stock in a low-inventory market.
- Price: £199/mo per postcode district (exclusive, max 2 agents/district); £49 per-lead pay-as-you-go option to reduce first-sale friction.
- Channel to first 10: Cold email + LinkedIn to letting agents scraped from Rightmove/Zoopla "Find an Agent" pages for 20 UK cities and the Propertymark public member directory, referencing Propertymark's own reported surge in full-management demand. £0 spend. (Does NOT rely on the 30K agent list; if sampling later proves that list is UK letting/estate agents rather than US realtors, it becomes an accelerant.)
- Supply seeding: Demand (landlord) side seeded founder-side, no wait: free RRA risk-scorer tool posted into landlord Facebook groups, Property118, LandlordZone, r/uklandlords, where RRA panic threads are live now; plus aggregation of public "should I switch to an agent / sell up" posts into week-1 lead volume. Agents buy against a delivered-leads SLA.
- Timing hypothesis: Renters' Rights Act provisions commenced 1 May 2026 (Section 21 abolished, periodic tenancies, £7,000/tenancy fines, info-sheet duty by 31 May 2026). Propertymark reports a marked increase in landlords requesting full management since February 2026, and compliance fatigue is pushing marginal landlords to sell — a brand-new, dated demand spike agents are competing to capture. Searchable: "Renters' Rights Act 1 May 2026 letting agents full management".
