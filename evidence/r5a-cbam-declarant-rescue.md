## R2 Timing Gate (2026-07-10) — PASS
- Definitive regime start 1 Jan 2026: VERIFIED. Importers must purchase/surrender CBAM certificates under the definitive regime from 1 Jan 2026 (https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en; https://www.dehst.de/EN/Topics/CBAM/CBAM-definitive-regime-2026/cbam-definitive-regime-2026_node.html).
- Omnibus 50t threshold: VERIFIED. Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 finally adopted 29 Sep 2025, in force Oct 2025; 50-tonne/yr de minimis replaces the €150 shipment test; exempts ~90% of importers while keeping ~99% of covered emissions (https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/eu-adopts-cbam-omnibus-regulation; https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-adopts-simplifications-cbam-rules-ahead-compliance-phase-starting-2026; https://cbamguide.com/compliance/de-minimis/).
- 31 Mar 2026 authorised-declarant deadline: VERIFIED. Importers/indirect customs reps who applied by 31 Mar 2026 may provisionally keep importing until the NCA decides (NCA has 120 days); if the application is later rejected, penalties apply retroactively to goods imported meanwhile (https://www.dehst.de/EN/Topics/CBAM/CBAM-definitive-regime-2026/CBAM-authorisation-definitive-regime/cbam-authorisation-definitive-regime_node.html; https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102lr9t/what-you-need-to-know-as-cbam-simplification-comes-into-effect/; https://www.mhc.ie/latest/insights/important-deadline-for-cbam-importers).
- What happens NOW to importers who missed it: import blocks are REAL and automated — without authorised-declarant status, customs systems automatically reject declarations once the importer exceeds/approaches the 50t threshold; no provisional-import right for late applicants; penalties harmonised with EU ETS at EUR 100/tCO2e embedded, and 3-5x that rate for importing without declarant status (https://www.customssupport.com/cbam-changes-in-2026/; https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead; https://www.epa.ie/our-services/licensing/climate-change/eu-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/).
- Certificates Feb 2027 / first declaration Sep 2027: VERIFIED. Certificate sales start 1 Feb 2027 on the central platform; first CBAM declaration + surrender (covering 2026 imports) due 30 Sep 2027, moved from 31 May (https://www.dehst.de/EN/Topics/CBAM/CBAM-definitive-regime-2026/CBAM-certificates/cbam-certificates_node.html; https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-adopts-simplifications-cbam-rules-ahead-compliance-phase-starting-2026).
- LIVE rescue moment July 2026: mechanism is live — unaware importers cross the cumulative 50t line mid-year and get auto-rejected at customs, then face an up-to-120-day wait with no right to import. BUT a targeted search found NO trade-press/NCA evidence of an actual stuck-importer backlog as of Jun-Jul 2026 (https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-registry-and-reporting_en; https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=97996896-1e1f-4b0e-a7f7-704dbe235775). Verdict: dates all correct, mechanism real → PASS, but timing scored 6 not higher: the panicked-cohort size is unevidenced and the Omnibus deliberately exempted ~90% of importers (the SME pool this offer targets).

- DELIVERY LEGALITY (decisive aggravator): the authorised CBAM declarant must be established in an EU Member State and hold an EU EORI; a non-EU party must act through an Indirect Customs Representative that is itself established in a Member State (https://www.epa.ie/our-services/licensing/climate-change/eu-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/; https://tulli.fi/en/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism; https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-registry-and-reporting_en). A UK (non-EU) solo founder therefore CANNOT be the declarant, cannot be an ICR, and cannot formally file: applications are made by the importer/ICR in the CBAM Registry of their Member State of establishment. The "rescue filing" component is only deliverable as ghost-prep of the importer's own submission — the offer as written overstates what a UK third party can legally execute. Not strictly impossible (advisory prep survives), but the jurisdictional/credibility gap vs. free NCA helpdesks and the importer's existing broker is unrebutted.
