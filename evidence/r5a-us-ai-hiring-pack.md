## Gate R4 — Red team (not reached; noted for the record)
Strongest failure case: US staffing agencies buying state-law "compliance packs" from an unlicensed UK individual is UPL-adjacent and trust-dead. Every state prohibits non-lawyers from practicing law; selling static forms is generally permitted, but customized/marketed-as-compliance legal documents sit on the wrong side of the line in several states (e.g. Missouri's static-form vs custom-tailored-document distinction; state bar UPL enforcement pages: https://www.calbar.ca.gov/public/concerns-about-attorney/avoid-legal-services-fraud/unauthorized-practice-law | https://www.michbar.org/file/professional/pdfs/uplfacts.pdf | https://www.osbar.org/UPL | https://www.adamsdrafting.com/legalzoom-and-unauthorized-practice-of-law/ — LegalZoom, a US company, spent years defending UPL suits over exactly this model). Agencies' E&O insurers and outside counsel steer compliance spend to Littler/Fisher Phillips/ASA/SixFifty, all of which carry licensed-attorney review the founder cannot claim.
Strongest rebuttal: position as "operational templates + regulatory trackers, not legal advice" with disclaimers (the forms exemption) — but that concedes the exact ground where ASA/law-firm free content already wins, and strips the only claim ($499 buys compliance) that justifies the price.
Defensible: FALSE.
